Beat 1 · The law

Selective Attention

People focus on what serves their goal and filter out the rest, often without realizing it.

Simons & Chabris (1999): about half of viewers counting basketball passes missed a person in a gorilla suit.

Source: lawsofux.com/selective-attention · Simons & Chabris, 1999

[~3 min] [If you have time, play the original “invisible gorilla” video before this slide and ask the room to count the passes. Then ask who saw the gorilla.] So, how many of you saw it? [Pause for hands] That study is Simons and Chabris, 1999. People were asked to count basketball passes, and about half of them completely missed a person in a gorilla suit walking through the middle of the scene. That’s Selective Attention: we focus on what serves our goal and filter out the rest, often without realizing we did it. [Point to the animation] Here’s the same effect on a web page. The spotlight is where attention goes as someone reads toward their goal: headline, text, image, button. Now look at those two flashing ads. They’re working so hard. And they never get a look. Quick note, the spotlight path is illustrative, not real eye-tracking data. Here’s the lesson for us: making something louder doesn’t mean it gets seen. If it looks like something people have learned to ignore, they’ll ignore it, no matter how bright it is. [Ask the room: “What’s something on a website you’ve trained yourself to never look at?”] [Let a few people answer. Expect cookie banners, ads, chat bubbles.] Let’s look at that in the wild.
