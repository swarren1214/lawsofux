[~2 min] Feel fast, even when it isn’t. Neither of these examples makes the network faster. They make the app feel faster. [Point to the left card] Instant likes. When you tap the heart in most social apps, it fills the moment you tap. The server catches up in the background. That’s called an optimistic update. In the rare case it fails, the app rolls it back and lets you know. Respond first, sync later. [Point to the right card] Skeleton screens. Apps like YouTube and LinkedIn show gray placeholder layouts while content loads instead of a blank page. You see the shape of what’s coming, so the wait feels shorter, and the page doesn’t jump around when the content lands. [Pause] Both of these are engineering patterns first, design second.
