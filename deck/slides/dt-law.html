Beat 1 · The law

Doherty Threshold

When a system responds in under about 400 milliseconds, people stay engaged and get more done.

Doherty & Thadani (1982, IBM): faster system response times led to sharply higher productivity.

Source: lawsofux.com/doherty-threshold · Doherty & Thadani, 1982

[~3 min] The Doherty Threshold. When a system responds in under about 400 milliseconds, people stay engaged and get more done. [Point to the animation] Both phones get the same tap on the heart. The slow one throws up a spinner and blocks the screen for over a second and a half. The fast one just responds. Timings are illustrative, but you can feel the difference just watching. This comes from a 1982 IBM paper by Walter Doherty and Ahrvind Thadani. They argued that productivity rises sharply once response time drops below about 400 milliseconds, because people stop losing their train of thought while they wait. [Pause] Engineers, here’s the practical point for you. Perceived speed matters as much as actual speed. A lot of the time you can respond instantly on screen and finish the real work in the background. [Ask the room: “What’s the slowest-feeling action in our product right now?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Write those down. Let’s see how other apps feel fast even when they aren’t.
