[~2 min] Pareto on Monday morning. Product, your question is “What are the vital few?” Before you set priorities, look at real data: usage by screen and feature, support tickets by cause, revenue by segment. The vital few are rarely where our gut says they are. Engineering, yours is “Where do errors cluster?” Crash reporting and error monitoring almost always follow a Pareto curve. Sort by frequency and impact, and fix from the top down. A handful of the right fixes will often do more for stability than months of scattered work. QA, your question is “Are we testing what’s used?” Test time is limited. So weight your coverage toward the paths people use most. Keep a baseline on everything else, but make sure the vital few get the most scrutiny. [Ask the room: “QA, does our current test plan match how people actually use the product, or how the product is organized?”] [Let one or two people answer.] Last law of the training, and it’s one you’ll recognize from every project you’ve ever worked on: Parkinson’s Law.
