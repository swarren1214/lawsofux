Beat 3 · At Kestrel (fictional)

Saved isn’t sent

Kestrel’s job report had a Save button that only stored the report on the phone.

Techs assumed Save meant the office had it. Reports sat on phones for days.

The fix: name the real states. “Saved on this phone” is different from “Sent to office.”

[~3 min] So picture a Kestrel tech wrapping up a job. They fill out the report, they tap Save, and they drive to the next house. Done, right? [Point to the Before screen] Except, not really. That Save button only stored the report on the phone. It never went to the office. And the reports sat on phones for days. [Pause] Here’s what I love about this one. To the engineer who built it, Save meant “write to local storage.” That was completely accurate. To the tech, Save was the last step of the job, so obviously the office had it. The word was correct. The model was wrong. And nobody did anything wrong, which is exactly why this kind of bug survives so long. [Point to the After screen] The fix changes what the screen shows. It tells the truth: “Saved on this phone, not sent.” And the main button is now “Send to office,” the thing the tech actually wants to do. Keeping it as a draft is still there, just secondary. Kestrel is fictional, but I promise every product has one of these. [Ask the room: “Which of our buttons mean something different to users than they do to us?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Let’s make it practical.
