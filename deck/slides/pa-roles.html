[~2 min] The Active User on Monday morning. Product, your question is “Where will they really learn?” Assume nobody reads the onboarding. If a feature only makes sense after you’ve watched the tour, the fix isn’t a better tour. The feature needs to change. Engineering, your question is “Is help in context?” And here’s the engineering reality: contextual help needs state. A tip has to know whether this person has already done the thing, show up once at the right moment, and then get out of the way. And empty states are real screens. They deserve real design and real implementation, not a placeholder someone forgot about. QA, your question is “Can you skip the tour?” Test the way new users actually behave. Skip every tour, dismiss every tip, and then try the core tasks. If you get stuck, that’s a bug, even if the tour would have explained it. [Ask the room: “QA, would anyone be comfortable filing that as a bug today? Why or why not?”] [Let one or two people answer.] Good, that’s exactly the conversation we want to have. And that’s the last law in Module 3. Let’s wrap it up.
