[~3 min] Two examples, both generic sketches. [Point to the left card] Tax software. Filing taxes is genuinely complicated. That’s intrinsic load, and no app can make it go away. What the interview format does is remove the extraneous load of reading a dense government form. One plain-language question per screen. Same work, less at once. [Point to the right card] And this one is for the engineers and QA folks especially: the dark cockpit. Airbus designed its overhead panels so that when everything is normal, the lights are off. So when a light does come on, it’s impossible to miss. It means something needs you. [Pause] Now compare that with a lot of dashboards you’ve seen, where every single metric is green, yellow or red all the time. When everything is lit up, nothing stands out. Silence is a feature. [Ask the room: “Where do we light everything up, alerts, badges, colors, so nothing stands out?”] [Let a few answer. Notification badges and status dashboards usually come up.] Okay, back to Kestrel, and the first screen their techs see every morning.
