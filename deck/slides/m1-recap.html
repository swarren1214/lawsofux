Module 1 recap

How people decide, in one slide.

01

Hick’s Law

More options, slower decisions. Hide the rare ones.

02

Choice Overload

Too many options and people choose nothing. Recommend one.

03

Cognitive Load

Spend people’s effort on the task, not the interface.

04

Working Memory

People hold about four things. Carry the rest for them.

05

Miller’s Law

A warning about memory, not a rule about menus.

06

Chunking

Group information so it can be scanned and recalled.

07

Selective Attention

People see what fits their goal. Don’t dress alerts as ads.

[~10 min] So that’s Module 1: how people decide. [Point to each card as you go] Hick’s Law, more options means slower decisions, so hide the rare ones. Choice Overload, too many options and people choose nothing, so recommend one. Cognitive Load, spend people’s effort on the task, not the interface. Working Memory, people hold about four things, so carry the rest for them. Miller’s Law, a warning about memory, not a rule about menus. Chunking, group information so it can be scanned and recalled. And Selective Attention, people see what fits their goal, so don’t dress alerts up as ads. [Pause] Before we wrap, I want each team to pick one of these laws and name one screen in our product where it applies. [Give the room a minute.] If we have time, let’s do a quick ten-minute exercise. Get into mixed groups, at least one person from Product, Engineering and QA in each. Pull up a real screen from our product and find as many Module 1 issues as you can. [Run the exercise, then ask each group to share their top find.] Here’s what usually happens: the groups find the same handful of screens. That overlap is a useful backlog all by itself, so let’s capture it. Next session is Module 2, where we move from how people decide to how they see: the Gestalt principles of grouping, plus Von Restorff and the Aesthetic-Usability Effect. Thanks, everyone.
