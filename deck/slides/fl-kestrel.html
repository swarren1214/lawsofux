[~3 min] So picture a Kestrel tech out on a job. They’re wearing gloves, they’re holding a sprayer in one hand and the phone in the other. Kestrel is fictional, but this pattern is everywhere: desktop habits carried straight onto a phone. [Point to the Before card] Here’s the job screen they had. The actions they use most are tiny icon buttons along the top edge. That’s the farthest point from a thumb holding the phone one-handed. And gloves make every target effectively smaller. So techs kept missing them, or hitting the wrong one. [Pause] [Point to the After card] Here’s Fitts’s Law in practice. The most frequent action, Complete step, is the biggest target, and it sits right at the bottom, where the thumb already rests. Photo and Note are still big, just less prominent. Nothing got removed. Things just moved closer and got bigger. [Ask the room: “Where do our most-used actions sit relative to the hand holding the phone?”] [Let two or three people answer.] That’s the question I want each team to take with them. Let’s make it specific.
