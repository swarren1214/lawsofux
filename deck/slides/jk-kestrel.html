Beat 3 · At Kestrel (fictional)

The swapped buttons

Kestrel’s booking flow put Continue on the left and Back on the right, the reverse of most apps.

Customers kept tapping Back by habit and lost their selections.

The fix: follow the convention. Back sits top-left; the primary action sits where people expect it.

[~3 min] So here’s Kestrel’s booking flow. A customer is picking an appointment time. [Point to the Before screen] And look at the bottom. Continue is on the left, Back is on the right. That’s the reverse of almost every app out there. Now, here’s what makes this a good story: somebody made that choice on purpose. Continue was the main action, so it went first. On paper, that’s a defensible decision. But customers don’t read buttons, they use habits. Their thumbs went straight to the familiar spot, they hit Back, and they lost their selection. Then they had to start over, and some of them probably didn’t. [Pause] [Point to the After screen] The fix is almost boring. Back moves to the top-left, where it lives in pretty much every app, and Continue is the big prominent button at the bottom. We didn’t invent anything clever. We just stopped fighting what people already know. Kestrel is fictional, but I’ve seen this exact bug in real products. [Ask the room: “Where have we invented our own pattern for something people already know how to do?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Let’s turn that into something each team can act on.
