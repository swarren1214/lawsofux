[~3 min] So picture a Kestrel tech halfway through a job. They’ve taken the before photo, they’ve done the inspection, and they’re about to start treatment. [Point to the Before screen] And then this pops up, full screen, right over the checklist: “Reminder: team lunch is on Friday.” They have to stop and tap OK. Now, nobody at Kestrel set out to make that annoying. The problem is the system treated a lunch reminder exactly the same as a safety alert. Every dispatch message interrupted, every time. Do that a few times a day and techs start losing their place. They come back to the checklist and skip a step. That’s how a notification turns into a quality problem. [Pause] [Point to the After screen] Here’s the fix. Routine messages collect behind that little badge, and the checklist stays exactly where the tech left it, with the current step clearly marked. And truly urgent messages still interrupt. We didn’t turn interruptions off, we made them earn it. Kestrel is fictional, but I bet this screen looks familiar. [Ask the room: “Which of our notifications interrupt people when they could just wait?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Okay, let’s make this practical for each team.
