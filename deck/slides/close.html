[~3 min] So that’s it. Five modules, thirty laws. [Point to the grid] Remember this grid from the very first slide? Thirty tiles, one for each law. Now they’re colored by module. Blue for how people decide. Purple for how people see. Green for how people act. Orange for how people remember. And pink for how we make trade-offs as a team. Seven, seven, five, seven and four. All thirty. [Pause] Here’s what I want you to take with you. Nobody needs to memorize these. I won’t be quizzing anyone. What matters is that Product, Engineering and QA can now look at the same screen and say, “that’s a Hick’s Law problem,” or “that alert has a Von Restorff issue,” and actually agree on what to do about it. That shared language is the whole point. Notice it, name it, fix it, together. If you want to go deeper, everything we covered is based on lawsofux.com by Jon Yablonski. It’s a great reference to keep bookmarked, and it goes further on each law than we had time for. [Ask the room: “Before we wrap, which law do you think you’ll notice first this week?”] [Let a few people answer, then close.] Thank you all for your time and for the great discussion across these sessions.
