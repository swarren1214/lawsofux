Let the screen do the remembering.

Visited links

[~2 min] Two examples, generic sketches again. [Point to the left card] Security code AutoFill. Remember how this used to work? You got a text with six digits, you read them, held them in your head, switched apps, and typed them in, hoping nobody interrupted you halfway. Since iOS 12, the iPhone just offers the code right above the keyboard. Nothing to memorize. The phone carries it across for you. [Point to the right card] Visited links. One of the oldest conventions on the web. Links you’ve already clicked show up in a different color, so you don’t have to remember where you’ve been. The interface remembers so you don’t have to. Both of these take a job away from working memory. Don’t make people carry it, and show the state, don’t ask for it. [Ask the room: “Where does our product make someone read something on one screen and type it on another?”] [Let a few answer. Order numbers, account IDs and codes from emails usually come up.] Those are all candidates. And Kestrel has a perfect example.
