[~10 min] So that’s Module 3, how people act. Five laws. [Point to the cards] Fitts’s Law: make frequent targets big and close to the hand. The Doherty Threshold: respond in under about 400 milliseconds and finish the rest in the background. Postel’s Law: accept messy input, store and show one clean format. Flow: protect focus, because every interruption has a recovery cost. And the Paradox of the Active User: nobody reads the manual, so teach at the moment of use. [Pause] Here’s something I want you to notice. Every one of these is decided, at least partly, in code. Hit-area sizes, response times, input parsing, notification priority, contextual help. Engineering, this is your module. So let’s try something. [Exercise] Pick one core task in our product. In small mixed groups, run it on a real phone, over a throttled network, one-handed, and skip all the onboarding. Write down every moment of friction and which law it maps to. You’ve got about seven minutes. [Let groups work, then ask each group for its top one.] Great.
