Beat 1 · The law

Postel’s Law

Be liberal in what you accept and conservative in what you send.

Jon Postel wrote it in 1980 as a rule for internet protocols. It works just as well for forms.

Source: lawsofux.com/postels-law · Postel, RFC 761, 1980

[~3 min] Postel’s Law. “Be liberal in what you accept and conservative in what you send.” It’s also called the robustness principle. Jon Postel wrote it in 1980, into an early specification for TCP, the protocol underneath most of the internet. It’s a big part of why the network tolerates so much variety. And it turns out it works just as well for forms. [Point to the animation] Watch the phone field. It gets typed five different ways: dashes, dots, spaces, a country code. And every single one ends up as the same clean stored value. [Pause] So here’s the translation for interfaces. Accept input the way people naturally give it, and turn it into one clean, predictable format yourself. People shouldn’t have to learn our database’s preferences. [Ask the room: “Has anyone ever been rejected by a form for typing a perfectly normal phone number?”] [Show of hands.] Yeah, almost everyone. Let’s look at two places where this gets done really well.
