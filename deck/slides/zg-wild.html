Beat 2 · In the wild

Unfinished things pull us back.

Profile strength

LinkedIn’s profile-strength meter shows how close you are to complete, with the next steps listed.

Show what’s left.

Continue Watching

Netflix puts unfinished shows front and center, each with a progress bar showing where you left off.

Make resuming effortless.

[~2 min] Two examples you’ve probably all seen. On the left, LinkedIn’s profile-strength meter. You see a bar that’s partly filled, and right under it, the specific items that would finish it. Add a photo, add a skill. The gap itself is the motivation. You see it’s not done, and they tell you exactly what’s left. On the right, Netflix’s Continue Watching row. Raise your hand if you’ve opened Netflix intending to start something new and ended up finishing a show instead. [Pause for hands] Right. That works because the unfinished episode is already on your mind. Netflix isn’t creating the pull, they’re just making it one tap away, and the progress bar shows exactly where you stopped. Make resuming effortless. [Pause] And here’s the ethical line again. Helping people finish something they started and want to finish is good design. Inventing incompleteness just to drive engagement is where this tips into manipulation. These are generic sketches, by the way, not real screenshots. Now let’s see what happens when unfinished work gets hidden instead.
