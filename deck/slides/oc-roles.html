[~2 min] Occam’s Razor on Monday morning. Product, your question is “What can we cut?” Try the removal test on a real screen. Take elements away one at a time and ask, does the main task still work? Anything that doesn’t break it is a candidate to cut, or at least to move further down. Engineering, yours is “Is the simple build enough?” Occam’s Razor applies to architecture just as much as interfaces. The simplest design that meets the real requirements is usually easier to test, cheaper to change and less likely to break. Building flexibility for some future need we might have someday has a real cost today. QA, your question is “What does extra cost?” Every toggle, option and variant multiplies the paths you have to test. And you’re often the first people to notice which options nobody uses. That’s really valuable input for cutting, so speak up when you see it. [Ask the room: “Engineering, where are we carrying complexity in the code for a feature almost nobody uses?”] [Let one or two people answer.] Now, if we’re going to cut, we need to know what matters most. That’s the Pareto Principle.
