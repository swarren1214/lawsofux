[~2 min] Product, your question is “Whose bias is this?” Because biases cut both ways. Our users have them, and so do we. Confirmation bias when we read research. Sunk-cost thinking about features we’ve already invested in. The false-consensus effect, assuming users think like us. So question our own assumptions as hard as we question the user’s. One easy habit: on a big decision, have someone argue the other side. Engineering, yours is “Is this nudge honest?” Engineers are often the last line of defense against dark patterns. You’re the ones actually building it. So it’s completely reasonable to push back on fake countdowns, pre-checked upsells, or confirmshaming copy, you know, the “No thanks, I don’t like saving money” button. QA, yours is “Would you feel tricked?” If you understood exactly how a flow works and you’d feel tricked, that’s a legitimate test. And a failing answer is a legitimate bug. File it. [Ask the room: “Do people here feel like they have permission to push back on this stuff?”] [Let two or three people answer. If the answer is no, say plainly that they do.] Alright, that’s all seven laws in Module 4. Let’s pull them together.
