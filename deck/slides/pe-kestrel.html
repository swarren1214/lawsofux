[~3 min] So at Kestrel, picture a great visit. The tech shows up on time, they’re friendly, they actually fix the problem. That’s the peak, and it’s a good one. The customer is happy. Then the tech leaves, and a little later the customer gets a text with the invoice. [Point to the Before card] And there’s a line on it nobody mentioned: a trip fee. [Pause] Now, what do you think the reviews talked about? Not the great service. The fee. The ending became the memory, and it wiped out everything good that came before it. Here’s what I like about the fix. It doesn’t hide the fee. Hiding it would just make things worse later. Instead it moves the price conversation to the beginning, where people expect to talk about money. Show the full price upfront. Then the ending gets redesigned as a summary of value. [Point to the After card] What we treated today, a confirmation that the total matches your quote, and a thank-you with a quick “How did we do?” Same visit, same price, very different memory. [Ask the room: “What’s the very last thing our customers see from us? Is that what we’d want them to remember?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Okay, let’s make it concrete for each team.
