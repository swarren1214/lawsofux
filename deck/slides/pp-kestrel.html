[~3 min] Here’s the situation at Kestrel. They’re planning a big redesign of their tech app, and the plan is: redesign all forty screens, same level of polish everywhere. [Point to the Before card] Same effort everywhere. And honestly, that plan feels good. It feels fair and thorough. But then someone actually pulled the usage data. It turned out six screens filled most of a tech’s day: Today, Job, Route, Photos, Report and Payment. A lot of the other thirty-four were rarely opened. Some once a quarter. So the even plan was spreading the team’s time across screens that barely mattered, while the screens techs live in all day got the same attention as the ones they almost never see. [Point to the After card] The fix is to polish the vital six first. They get the deepest research, design and testing. The other thirty-four get a lighter touch later. And some of them might just get removed, which is Occam’s Razor and Pareto working together. Kestrel is fictional, but this happens in real roadmaps all the time. [Ask the room: “If we ranked our screens by real use, would our roadmap look the same as it does now?”] [Let two or three people answer. Be honest if the answer is no.] Let’s get specific for each team.
