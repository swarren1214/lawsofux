[~2 min] Your Monday questions for working memory. [Point to Product] Product, ask “what must they remember?” Walk each core flow and write down everything the user has to hold in their head from one screen to the next. That list is your backlog. Every item on it is something we could carry for them. [Point to Engineering] Engineering, ask “can we carry it forward?” Autofill, keep context visible, and above all, preserve state. A form that clears when you tap Back is forcing someone to remember and re-enter everything. State preservation is a working-memory feature, even if it doesn’t sound like one. [Point to QA] QA, ask “does Back lose my work?” Interruption testing is underrated. Go back, switch apps, let the phone lock, take a call in the middle of a form. If anything the user typed disappears, that’s a working-memory bug, and it should be filed like one. [Ask the room: “Has anyone lost a half-finished form in our product?”] [Wait for the groans.] Yep. Next is probably the most famous, and most misquoted, law in this whole deck: Miller’s Law.
