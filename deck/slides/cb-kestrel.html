[~3 min] So this is the uncomfortable side of cognitive bias. Once you understand scarcity, urgency and anchoring, it gets really easy to exploit them. At Kestrel, someone wanted to boost bookings. [Point to the Before card] So they added “Only 2 slots left today!” and a countdown timer to every booking page. Every single one. [Pause] And it worked, for a little while. Bookings went up. Because those are real biases, and they really do trigger action. Then people noticed the banner never changed. It always said two slots. The timer always reset. Complaints came in, and cancellations followed. That’s the pattern with this kind of thing. It works short term, then it erodes trust as soon as people catch on. And these tactics are getting more and more scrutiny from regulators as dark patterns. [Point to the After card] The fix is honestly pretty simple. Show real availability. Update it as bookings come in. And if slots really are running out, say so. Only create urgency when it’s true. [Ask the room: “Is there anything in our product that only works because people don’t notice how it works?”] [Pause and let that one sit. Take an answer or two.] Okay. Let’s take that to each team.
