Beat 1 · The law

Uniform Connectedness

Things joined by a line or frame look more related than things that aren’t.

Palmer & Rock (1994): a connecting line can override both color and spacing as a grouping cue.

Source: lawsofux.com/law-of-uniform-connectedness · Palmer & Rock, 1994

[~3 min] Uniform Connectedness. Things joined by a line or a frame look more related than things that aren’t. [Point to the animation] Watch the dots before any lines show up. Everyone groups them by color, blues with blues, oranges with oranges. That’s similarity, the law we just did. [Wait for the lines to draw in] Now five lines draw in, each one joining a blue to an orange, and the grouping flips. Connection beats similarity. That’s from Palmer and Rock in 1994: a connecting line can override both color and spacing as a grouping cue. [Pause] It’s why steppers, timelines, flow diagrams and connected toggles work so well. A line tells people “these go together, and probably in this order.” The catch is the same as with boxes. A stray divider or decorative line can tie together things that have nothing to do with each other, and people will believe it. [Ask the room: “Where have you seen a line on a screen that was just decoration but made things look connected?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Let’s see this in a map almost everyone recognizes.
