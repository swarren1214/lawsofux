Beat 1 · The law

Cognitive Load

Every screen spends some of a limited supply of mental effort. Spend it on the task, not the interface.

Sweller (1988): intrinsic load comes from the task itself. Extraneous load comes from how it’s presented.

Source: lawsofux.com/cognitive-load · Sweller, 1988

[~3 min] Cognitive Load. Every screen spends some of a limited supply of mental effort, and the goal is to spend it on the task, not on the interface. [Point to the animation] Watch the phone. Every new element on its own is reasonable. A promo banner, some stats, a map, messages. But watch the meter climb. And watch the one thing the user actually came for, the next job, get pushed down and finally off the screen. And then, of course, a pop-up lands on top. [Pause] The animation is illustrative, not measured data, but I bet it looks familiar. The key idea here comes from John Sweller in 1988, and it’s a distinction worth memorizing. Intrinsic load is the real difficulty of the task. If the task is hard, it’s hard, and we can’t remove that. Extraneous load is everything we add on top of it: clutter, inconsistency, unclear labels. That part is entirely ours. We created it, and we can take it away. [Ask the room: “On our busiest screen, how much is the task and how much is us?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Let’s see two products that took the extraneous load away.
