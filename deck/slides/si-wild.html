Same look, same job.

Blue underlined links

For decades, blue underlined text has meant “clickable” everywhere on the web, wherever it appears.

One look, one meaning.

US road signs

Warning signs share a yellow diamond; regulatory signs share a white rectangle. You know the family first.

Families should look alike.

[~2 min] Two examples, one on screen and one on the road. [Point to the left card] Blue underlined links. Honestly, this might be the most successful similarity convention in computing. For decades, wherever blue underlined text shows up, people know it’s clickable before they read a single word. And notice how annoyed you get when it isn’t clickable. That annoyance is the promise breaking. [Point to the right card] US road signs take the same idea up a level in stakes. Warning signs share a yellow diamond. Regulatory signs share a white rectangle. You know what family a sign belongs to before you can read a word on it, and at highway speed that matters. These illustrations are generic sketches, by the way. [Ask the room: “Where does our product use one style to mean two different things, or two different styles to mean the same thing?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Hold onto those. Kestrel has one that ended up texting customers by mistake.
