Module 2 recap

How people see, in one slide.

01

Proximity

Space groups things. Keep labels close to what they label.

02

Common Region

A shared container makes a unit, even across gaps.

03

Similarity

Things that look alike should act alike.

04

Uniform Connectedness

A line links things more strongly than space or color.

05

Prägnanz

People see the simplest shape. Design for a glance.

06

Von Restorff

The one that’s different gets remembered. Ration it.

07

Aesthetic-Usability

Beauty buys forgiveness. Don’t let it hide problems.

[~10 min] So here’s Module 2 on one slide: how people see. [Pause] I want to point out something about the first five, Proximity, Common Region, Similarity, Uniform Connectedness and Prägnanz. They’re really one idea. Your brain groups things before it reads them. It uses spacing, containers, how things look, what’s connected and the simplest shape it can find. And here’s the important bit: any of those can contradict the words on the screen. When they do, the grouping usually wins. Then Von Restorff: the thing that’s different gets remembered, so ration it. And Aesthetic-Usability: beauty buys forgiveness, so don’t let it hide problems. Okay, let’s do a quick exercise. [Split into mixed groups of Product, Engineering and QA.] Each group gets one real screen from our product. Take five minutes and mark every group you see. Circle them, draw lines, whatever. Then compare what you found with what the screen is supposed to say. [Give them five minutes, then ask each group for their biggest mismatch.] [Let each group share.] Those mismatches are a ready-made backlog. Next session is Module 3: how people act. We go from seeing to doing, with Fitts’s Law, the Doherty Threshold, Postel’s Law, Flow and the Paradox of the Active User. Thanks, everyone.
