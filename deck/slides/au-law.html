Beat 1 · The law

Aesthetic-
Usability Effect

People see attractive designs as easier to use, and forgive their small flaws.

Kurosu & Kashimura (1995): ATM layouts rated more beautiful were also rated easier to use.

Source: lawsofux.com/aesthetic-usability-effect · Kurosu & Kashimura, 1995

[~3 min] Last law of the module, and it’s a fun one: the Aesthetic-Usability Effect. People believe good-looking designs are easier to use, and they forgive small flaws in them. [Point to the booking card] Watch the card on the right. Same features, same steps, nothing added or removed. All that changes is the craft: alignment, spacing, type, color. And notice how the star rating moves with it. Those stars are illustrative, by the way, not data. Where does this come from? In 1995, two researchers at Hitachi, Kurosu & Kashimura, had people rate ATM screen layouts. The layouts people found more beautiful were also rated easier to use. [Pause] So two takeaways. One: polish isn’t decoration. It literally changes how forgiving people are when something goes wrong. Two, and QA, I want you to hear this one: a beautiful design can make someone tell you it’s easy while they’re struggling. [Ask the room: “Has anyone ever shipped something pretty that tested great and then flopped?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Keep that story in mind. Let’s look at both sides of this out in the wild.
