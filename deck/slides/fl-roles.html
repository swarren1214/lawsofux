[~2 min] Fitts’s Law on Monday morning. Product folks, your question is “How far is the main action?” Map your most frequent actions against where they sit on screen. The most common one should be the biggest and the closest to the hand. If it’s tucked in a top corner, that’s a conversation. Engineering, “Is the hit area big enough?” The visible size of an icon and the size of its tappable area are two separate decisions. Padding a 24-point icon out to a 44-point hit area costs you nothing visually, and it fixes a ton of mis-taps. Also watch for targets crammed too close together. QA, “Can you hit it one-handed?” Emulators and mice hide Fitts’s Law problems completely. Test on real devices, with one hand, in the conditions our users actually work in. Gloves, if that’s their world. [Ask the room: “QA, how much of our testing happens on a real phone, one-handed?”] [Let a couple of people answer.] Okay. Hitting the button is half of it. What happens after the tap is the Doherty Threshold.
