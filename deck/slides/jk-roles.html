[~2 min] Jakob’s Law on Monday morning. Product, your question is “How do other apps do this?” Before you design a common interaction, look at how three or four well-known products handle it. If they all agree, that’s your starting point. Any deviation needs to earn its keep. Engineering, your question is “Are we using native controls?” Native and platform-standard components come with years of learned behavior built right in: gestures, keyboard handling, accessibility, focus order. When we build a custom replacement, we have to rebuild all of that ourselves, and usually we don’t. QA, your question is “Would this surprise anyone?” And honestly, you all have a superpower here. Testers tend to be heavy users of lots of other apps, which makes you great at spotting a broken convention. So trust that “huh, that’s weird” instinct, and log it, even if it matches the spec. [Ask the room: “Engineering, where have we built a custom control that the platform already gives us?”] [Let one or two people answer.] Alright. Jakob’s Law is about what people expect from other products. Next, let’s talk about what they expect from ours: Mental Models.
