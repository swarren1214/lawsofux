Beat 3 · At Kestrel (fictional)

The “Close Job” screen

Kestrel techs close every visit on a phone, in a driveway, often wearing gloves. Over three years, every team added its own button.

Techs stalled, tapped “Other,” and called dispatch to sort it out.

The fix: one primary action, two common alternates, and everything else behind “More.”

[~3 min] So picture a Kestrel tech at the end of a visit. They’re standing in a customer’s driveway, phone in one hand, probably still wearing gloves, and they need to close the job. [Point to the animation and let it loop once] Watch the left side. Over three years, every team at Kestrel added its own button to this screen. Twelve buttons, all the same size, all the same weight. The thumb hunts around, and where does it land? “Other.” And then the tech calls dispatch to sort it out. [Pause] Here’s the thing I want you to notice. None of those buttons was a bad idea. Every one of them had a sponsor and a good reason. The failure was that nobody owned the total. Now look at the right side. One primary action, two common alternates, and everything else behind “More.” Nothing was deleted: photo, note, warranty, they all still live under More. And that primary button is big enough to hit with a gloved thumb, which is a little preview of Fitts’s Law in Module 3. [Ask the room: “What’s our Close Job screen? Which screen in our product has grown a button every quarter?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Good. So what do we each actually do about it?
