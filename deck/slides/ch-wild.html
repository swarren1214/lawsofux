Grouping is a design decision.

Card numbers

Cards print 16 digits as four groups of four. Good checkout forms format them the same way as you type.

Match the printed format.

Wikipedia articles

Headings, a contents list and a summary box let readers jump straight to the part they need.

Headings are handles.

[~2 min] Card numbers are the cleanest everyday example. [Point to the left card] Sixteen digits that nobody could hold at once become four manageable groups of four. And here’s the part for the engineers in the room: good checkout forms match that grouping as you type and when you paste. When a field strips the spaces, or makes you type sixteen digits in a row, people end up checking their card digit by digit. You’ve all done it. That’s a chunking failure, and it’s really easy to fix. [Point to the right card] Wikipedia shows chunking at the page level. Same content, but headings, a table of contents and a summary box let you jump straight to the part you need. Nobody reads a Wikipedia article top to bottom. Those headings are handles.
