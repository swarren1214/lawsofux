[~3 min] So why are we doing this at all? Because every screen we ship runs on someone else’s brain, and that brain has limits we can’t patch. I want to be clear about one thing up front: the goal is not to turn engineers and testers into designers. It’s to give all three teams the same vocabulary for problems everyone already feels. [Point to the Product card] Product, your biggest lever is what you leave out. “Just one more option” always costs something. [Point to the Engineering card] Engineering, latency, tap target sizes, how input gets handled: those are UX decisions, and they get made in code, usually by you. [Point to the QA card] And QA, you’ve all seen a build that passes every test case and is still painful to use. Here’s why shared language helps. When QA can write “this breaks the Doherty Threshold” instead of “it feels slow,” that bug gets fixed faster. When an engineer knows Fitts’s Law, the tiny icon button gets questioned before it ships, not after. [Ask the room: “What’s a bug you’ve filed or received that just said ‘feels off’?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Keep those in mind. Here’s how we’ll get through all thirty.
