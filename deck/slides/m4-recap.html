Module 4 recap

How people remember, in one slide.

01

Jakob’s Law

People expect your product to work like the ones they already use.

02

Mental Model

People act on their picture of how it works. Show the real one.

03

Serial Position

First and last are remembered. Don’t bury what matters.

04

Peak-End Rule

The best or worst moment and the ending become the memory.

05

Zeigarnik Effect

Unfinished tasks stick. Make resuming easy, and honest.

06

Goal-Gradient

Effort rises near the goal. Show progress and give credit.

07

Cognitive Bias

Framing and defaults steer decisions. Use them honestly.

[~8 min] So that’s Module 4. Let’s zoom out for a second, because there’s a thread running through all seven of these. People don’t experience our product fresh. They arrive with habits and expectations from everything else they use. That’s Jakob’s Law and mental models. Then they leave with a memory, and that memory is shaped by order, by peaks, by endings, and by what’s still unfinished. That’s serial position, peak-end, Zeigarnik and goal-gradient. And in between, biases bend their judgment. And ours. [Pause] I want to say this one more time, because it matters more in this module than any other. Several of these laws can be weaponized. Peak-end, Zeigarnik, goal-gradient and especially cognitive bias can all be used to trick people. Let’s not do that. Now, if we have time, a quick exercise. [Split into mixed groups of Product, Engineering and QA. Give them about five minutes.] Pick one real flow in our product. Mark four things: its peak, its ending, anything important that’s buried in the middle, and any nudge that wouldn’t survive a user understanding how it works. [Bring the groups back and have each one share its most surprising find.] Great. Next time is Module 5, the final one, and it turns the lens on us as a team. Tesler’s Law, Occam’s Razor, the Pareto Principle and Parkinson’s Law. Expect some debate. Thanks, everyone.
