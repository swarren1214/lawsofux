[~2 min] Bigger and closer wins. Two examples. [Point to the left card] Screen edges. This is the classic one. The Mac menu bar sits on the top edge of the screen, and the cursor can’t go past that edge. So you can throw your mouse upward without aiming and you’ll land on it. That makes the target effectively infinitely tall. Corners are even better, because they stop you in two directions. Edges are free targets. [Point to the right card] On touch screens there’s no edge trick, so size has to do the work. Apple’s design guidelines have long recommended tap targets of at least 44 by 44 points, about a fingertip. Google’s Material guidelines suggest 48 by 48 density-independent pixels. And here’s the nuance: the icon you see can be smaller. It’s the tappable area that needs to be generous. These illustrations are generic sketches.
