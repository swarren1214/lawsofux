[~3 min] So at Kestrel, and again, Kestrel is our fictional company, new techs had to memorize twelve numeric status codes just to update dispatch. [Point to the Before screen] Picture a tech who just pulled into a driveway, phone in one hand, clipboard in the other, and the app is asking for a code. Was 07 “arrived” or “en route”? Under pressure, people mix them up. And then dispatch spends its afternoon chasing delays that never happened. Where did these codes come from? Numeric codes are everywhere in field operations. They’re inherited from radio dispatch, back when bandwidth was a voice channel and short codes made total sense. On a touchscreen, though, they force recall when recognition is free. [Point to the After screen] The fix is just to show the statuses in words. And notice it also adds order: the statuses follow the job’s natural sequence, and the next likely one is highlighted. The codes didn’t disappear. They still live in the database for reporting. They just never have to live in a technician’s head. [Ask the room: “Where do we make people remember a code, an ID or a sequence the screen could just show?”] [Let a few people answer.] Okay, let’s make this concrete for each team.
