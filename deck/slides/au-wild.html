The Hitachi ATM study

Looks shape trust.

Pretty can hide problems

Nielsen Norman Group warns that attractive designs can mask usability issues in user testing.

Measure, don’t just ask.

[~2 min] The headline says it all: beauty buys trust, and it hides bugs. Let’s take those one at a time. [Point to the left card] This is the Hitachi ATM study I just mentioned, from Masaaki Kurosu and Kaori Kashimura. The striking part is the timing. People rated the better-looking layouts as easier to use before they’d even tried them. So looks set expectations before anyone taps a thing. [Point to the right card] Now the warning label. Nielsen Norman Group has written about how attractive designs make test participants more forgiving. They blame themselves for mistakes. They say “oh, that was easy” while they’re actually failing the task. [Pause] So for anyone running research or QA on a polished product: satisfaction ratings on their own can’t be trusted. You have to measure what people do, not just ask how they feel. Quick note, these illustrations are generic sketches, not real screenshots.
