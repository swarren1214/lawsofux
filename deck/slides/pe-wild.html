Design the ending on purpose.

IKEA’s exit

IKEA ends a long, maze-like store visit with cheap food and treats right by the exit.

End on a high.

[~2 min] Two examples, one digital and one physical. On the left, Mailchimp. For years, after you sent a campaign, they showed this little high-five animation. And it became one of the most talked-about details in product design. Why would a high five matter that much? Because hitting send to thousands of people is nerve-racking. That’s the peak, and it’s a scary one. Mailchimp turned the ending into a tiny celebration. Celebrate the finish. On the right, IKEA, which is the go-to physical example for this rule. Let’s be honest, an IKEA trip can be long and tiring. It’s a maze. But where does it end? Cheap food and treats right by the exit. End on a high. [Pause] Notice that neither of them removed the hard parts. The send is still scary. The maze is still a maze. They just made sure the last thing you feel is good. Quick note, these are generic sketches, not real screenshots. [Ask the room: “What’s the very last screen people see in our core flows? And how does it feel?”] [Let a few people answer.] Keep that in mind, because at Kestrel the ending went the other way.
