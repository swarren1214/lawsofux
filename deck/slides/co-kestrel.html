[~3 min] So now picture a homeowner who just found a spider in the kitchen and wants to sign up with Kestrel. They land on the sign-up page, and they get this. [Point to the Before card] Nine service plans: Basic, Basic Plus, Standard, Standard Plus, and so on, each with its own optional add-ons, all on one screen. Every one of those plans existed for a reason. A sales experiment, a regional variant, one customer who asked once. Together they turned sign-up into homework. So what did customers do? They compared, they gave up, and they called the office to ask which one to buy. [Pause] Now look at the fix. [Point to the After card] Three plans, and one marked “Most popular.” Notice what the fix does and doesn’t do. It doesn’t delete the add-ons. It moves them to after sign-up, when the customer has already committed and it’s easier to evaluate extras. And it doesn’t just shrink the list. It marks a recommended default, and that default is what actually breaks the paralysis. [Ask the room: “Where in our product do we make people compare things before they’ve even committed?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Good. So here’s what each of us does about it.
