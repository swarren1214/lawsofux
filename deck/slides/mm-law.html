Mental Model

People build a working picture of how a system works, then act on that picture.

Norman (1988): good design lines up the designer’s model, the system as shown, and the user’s model.

Source: lawsofux.com/mental-model · Norman, 1988

[~3 min] Mental Models. Everybody who uses our product carries a little picture in their head of how it works, and they act on that picture, whether it’s right or not. [Point to the animation] Watch the first run. The user deletes a document on their phone. In their head, it’s gone from that phone. But their files are synced, so it disappears from the laptop and the tablet too. [Pause] Now here’s the key thing. Nothing malfunctioned. The system did exactly what it was built to do. It just didn’t match the picture in the user’s head. Don Norman wrote about this in 1988. He describes three models: the designer’s model, the user’s model, and what he calls the “system image,” which is everything the interface actually shows. And the user only ever sees the system image. They never see our architecture diagram. So that’s where the real model has to be made visible. [Wait for the second run] Watch the second run. One honest question, “Delete from all your devices?”, and the gap closes. [Ask the room: “What’s something in our product that works exactly as designed but still surprises people?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Let’s look at one model that works beautifully and one that people get wrong.
