[~3 min] So picture a Kestrel tech pulling up to 88 Oak Street, a little behind schedule, thumb already moving before the screen finishes loading. [Point to the Before card] The visit screen had two buttons stacked on top of each other, both the same blue: Confirm visit and Cancel visit. The only difference between them was the label. And people moving fast don’t read labels. They hit the blue button where they expect it. So techs canceled visits they meant to confirm, and customers got cancellation texts for appointments that were actually happening. [Pause] That’s a pretty bad phone call to get. [Point to the After card] The fix: destructive actions get their own look. Cancel becomes red and outlined, a style that only destructive actions ever use. The ellipsis on “Cancel visit…” tells you another step is coming, and the confirm step catches whatever slips through. Similarity says these two things are the same kind of thing, so we made sure they look different. [Ask the room: “Where do our safe actions and our destructive actions share the same button style?”] [Let a few people answer.] Okay, let’s make this practical for each team.
