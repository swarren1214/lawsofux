[~2 min] Two places where people use the ends on purpose. On the left, navigation bars. Look at how almost every site is laid out. The logo, which doubles as the home link, sits first. The account, the cart or the main call to action sits last. Those are the positions people remember best, and the links in the middle are the ones people usually have to scan for. [Pause] On the right, Apple keynotes. Under Steve Jobs, they often saved a big reveal for the very end with “One more thing…”. It became a signature because that final moment was the one everybody remembered and repeated the next day. That’s the recency effect, used on purpose. And this applies to our own work too, not just the product. Onboarding, release notes, sprint demos, and yes, this training. Put what matters at the start and the end. Quick note, these illustrations are generic sketches. [Ask the room: “Think about our last sprint demo. What did we put last?”] [Let a couple of people answer.] Okay, now one where the middle actually matters for safety. Let’s go to Kestrel.
