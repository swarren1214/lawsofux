[~3 min] This one’s about Kestrel’s invoice. Fictional company, but I bet this invoice looks familiar. [Point to the Before screen] Every line item in one long column, one total at the bottom. So picture a customer standing at their front door holding this, and they ask the tech, “What am I actually paying for?” The tech scrolls, squints, starts reading line by line. Customers kept calling in with that same question, and techs couldn’t explain it quickly. [Point to the After screen] Now look at the fix. Same line items. Same total. Nothing removed. All we did was group them into Service, Materials and Fees, each with its own name, color and subtotal. Now the customer can answer “what am I paying for?” in three glances instead of ten lines. And the conversation changes too. It becomes, “The materials were higher this time because we used more product,” instead of line-by-line archaeology. That’s chunking applied to a document. [Ask the room: “Which of our screens or exports is one long list that should really be three short ones?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Alright, let’s take it to each team.
