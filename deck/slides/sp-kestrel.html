Beat 3 · At Kestrel (fictional)

The safety checklist

Kestrel’s pre-job safety checklist had twelve items. The most critical one sat seventh.

In spot checks, techs recalled the first and last items, and missed the one in the middle.

The fix: put the critical item first, repeat it last, and trim the list in between.

Before

Safety check

1. Wear gloves

2. Check the wind

3. Read the label

4. Close windows

5. Cover fish tanks

6. Note the drains

7. Clear kids and pets

8. Check the hose

+ 4 more

After

Safety check

Clear kids and pets

Gloves on

Wind and weather

Read the label

Windows and tanks

Area still clear?

[~3 min] This Kestrel story is a little more serious, because it’s about real-world safety. Before every job, the tech runs through a safety checklist. [Point to the Before screen] Twelve items: gloves, wind, read the label, close the windows, cover the fish tanks, and so on. Every single item matters, so they were listed in no particular order. And the most critical one, “Clear kids and pets,” ended up at number seven. Right in the middle, exactly where memory is weakest. In spot checks, techs remembered the first and last items and missed the one in the middle. [Pause] [Point to the After screen] Here’s the fix, and it uses both ends of that U-curve. The critical item goes first, and it comes back at the end as the final confirmation: “Area still clear?” And the middle got trimmed by grouping related checks, windows and tanks together, for example. That’s Miller’s Law and chunking from Module 1 showing up again. Kestrel is fictional, but plenty of real checklists look like that before screen. [Ask the room: “In our most important lists, what’s sitting in the middle that really shouldn’t be?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Let’s turn that into action for each team.
