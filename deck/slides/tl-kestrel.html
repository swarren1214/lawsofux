[~3 min] Back to Kestrel, our fictional home-services company. When a new customer signed up, Kestrel asked them to choose their local branch. [Point to the Before card] From a list of fourteen. North Valley, South Valley, Central, East Bench, and so on. And there’s a little “Not sure? Call us” at the bottom, which tells you everything. So what happened? Customers guessed. They picked wrong. And then they got scheduled with a tech who was two towns away. Here’s the thing: the branch boundaries really are complicated. They follow service routes, not city lines, and they change as the company grows. That complexity is real. You can’t delete it. Tesler’s Law just asks who carries it. [Point to the After card] In the fix, the customer types their address, and the system looks up the branch. “Served by Central.” They just confirm it. So one engineer maintaining a lookup table replaces thousands of customers guessing. And notice the “Not right? Change branch” link. That’s the escape hatch for edge cases, because the system won’t be right every time. [Ask the room: “What part of our internal structure, our teams, regions, plan types, are we making customers understand?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Now let’s make this concrete for each team.
