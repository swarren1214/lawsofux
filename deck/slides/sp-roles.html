[~2 min] Serial Position on Monday morning. Product, your question is “What goes first and last?” Treat position as a priority decision, not an accident. In menus, settings lists, onboarding steps, release notes, put the thing you most need people to remember at the start or the end. Engineering, your question is “Does the order hold?” Here’s the trap. Product carefully orders a list, and then it gets scrambled by alphabetical sorting, async loading or personalization. If the order is a product decision, protect it in code and cover it with tests. QA, your question is “What do people recall?” Try a simple recall check. After someone walks through a flow or a list, ask them what they remember. If the critical item is consistently missing from their answer, it’s probably buried in the middle. [Ask the room: “Engineering, do we have any lists where the order changes depending on how fast things load?”] [Let one or two people answer.] Good. So Serial Position is about the order of things in a list. Next we’ll look at the order of moments in an experience, and why the end matters so much: the Peak-End Rule.
