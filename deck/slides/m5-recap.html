[~10 min] So here’s Module 5 on one slide. Tesler’s Law: complexity only moves, so let the system carry it. Occam’s Razor: choose the simplest option that still works and cut the rest. The Pareto Principle: a vital few drive most of the results, so count, then focus. And Parkinson’s Law: work expands to fill the time, so fix the time and flex the scope. [Pause] You might have noticed these pull against each other. Tesler’s Law argues for more engineering effort. Occam’s Razor and Parkinson’s Law argue for less. Pareto is the tiebreaker: put the effort where it matters most. That tension is exactly why Product and Engineering argue about this stuff, and now we have shared words for the argument. [Point to the gradient card] Now, the homework, and this is for the whole training, not just today. Each team picks one law, any of the thirty, and finds it in our product this sprint. Product, bring one screen where a law is being broken. Engineering, bring one fix that moves complexity off users. QA, bring one test that would have caught it. We’ll go through them at the next planning session. [Exercise, ~5 min: in mixed groups of Product, Engineering and QA, pick your law and your screen right now so nobody leaves without a starting point.] Thirty laws is a lot. One real fix per team is a great start. Okay, one last slide.
