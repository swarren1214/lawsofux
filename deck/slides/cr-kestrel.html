[~3 min] Back to Kestrel, our fictional home-services company. Picture a tech first thing in the morning, coffee in one hand, phone in the other, scanning the day’s schedule. [Point to the Before card] The schedule was one long run of lines: time, address, notes, time, address, notes. Look at “Gate locked, call first.” It sits exactly as close to the 9:30 visit as it does to the 8:00. So which house has the locked gate? Under time pressure, people attach the note to whichever address they happen to be looking at. And that’s what happened. Techs read a gate note and applied it to the wrong house. [Pause] [Point to the After card] The fix is just Common Region. Each visit gets its own card, so a note can only belong to the visit it sits inside. It physically can’t drift to the neighbor. And notice the notes also share one consistent style, which is a little preview of the next law. [Ask the room: “Where do we show lists of records where a detail could be read as belonging to the row next to it?”] [Let a few people answer.] Alright, let’s turn this into something each team can actually do.
