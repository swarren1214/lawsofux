Cut to the core.

[~2 min] Two examples of Occam’s Razor out in the world. On the left, Dieter Rams. He was Braun’s head of design for decades, and he summed up his whole approach in three words: “Less, but better.” And notice, that isn’t a style. It’s not “make it white and minimal.” It means take away everything that doesn’t serve the person using the product. On the right is the software version, and it’s a story some of you may know. Instagram started as an app called Burbn. Burbn let you check in to places, make plans, earn points, and share photos. Busy app. The founders noticed that photo sharing was the thing people actually used. So they cut everything else and relaunched. [Pause] Here’s what I want you to take from that. Occam’s Razor rarely means building a less ambitious product. It means finding the part that really matters and letting it be clear. And as always, these illustrations are generic sketches. [Ask the room: “If we had to cut our product down to the one thing people really use it for, what would it be?”] [Let a few people answer.
