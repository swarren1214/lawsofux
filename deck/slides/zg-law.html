Beat 1 · The law

Zeigarnik Effect

People remember unfinished or interrupted tasks better than finished ones.

Zeigarnik (1927) found people recalled interrupted tasks better than completed ones.

Source: lawsofux.com/zeigarnik-effect · Zeigarnik, 1927

[~3 min] This one has a great origin story. Psychologist Kurt Lewin was sitting in a restaurant and noticed the waiter remembered every unpaid order in perfect detail. But the moment an order was paid, it was gone. The waiter couldn’t tell you a thing about it. His student, Bluma Zeigarnik, tested this in 1927 and found people recalled interrupted tasks better than completed ones. So that’s the law: unfinished things stick in our heads. [Point to the animation] Watch the list on the right. That one unfinished step keeps tugging at you. And once it’s done, the whole list just fades. Your brain lets go of it. Products use this constantly: progress indicators, “continue where you left off,” that sort of thing. [Pause] Now, a caution, and I want to say it out loud. This one can be abused. Endless nags. Fake incompleteness. “Your profile is only 60% complete!” when nobody needs that last 40%. Use it to help people finish what they actually want to finish.
