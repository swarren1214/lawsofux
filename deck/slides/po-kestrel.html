Beat 3 · At Kestrel (fictional)

The phone field

Kestrel’s sign-up form rejected any phone number with dashes, spaces or parentheses.

Customers saw “Invalid format,” couldn’t tell what was wrong, and gave up.

The fix: accept any format, strip it to digits, and store and show one clean version.

[~3 min] So picture a new Kestrel customer signing up. Kestrel is fictional, but this is one of the most common form bugs on the web. [Point to the Before card] They type their mobile number exactly the way it’s written everywhere else in their life: (801) 555-0142. Parentheses, a space, a dash. And the form says “Invalid format.” That’s it. No hint about what’s wrong. They try a couple of times, can’t figure it out, and give up. We just lost a customer at the phone field. [Pause] [Point to the After card] Here’s the fix. The form accepts whatever they type, here it’s dots, strips out the punctuation and shows them how it’ll be saved: “Saved as (801) 555-0142.” Now, validation doesn’t go away. If someone types too few digits, they get a specific, helpful message telling them what’s missing. We just stop rejecting things that are obviously fine. [Ask the room: “Which of our fields are stricter than they need to be?”] [Let two or three people answer.] Let’s turn that into something each team can do.
