[~2 min] The theme here is that the fix for choice overload is almost never “delete options.” It’s defaults, recommendations and structure. [Point to Product] Product, your question is “what should be the default?” Every configuration screen should have an answer to “what would we pick for most people?” Recommend that one. Let people change it later. [Point to Engineering] Engineering, ask “can it start pre-filled?” Defaults, sort order and filters are features. They need to be designed and built, not bolted on at the end. A longer list is rarely the answer. [Point to QA] And QA, “can a newcomer choose alone?” This is one law where a hallway test beats a test case. Grab someone who has never seen the screen, hand it to them, and stay quiet. Note every stall and every time they go back. [Ask the room: “Who knows a screen in our product with no default at all?”] [Take one or two answers.] So far we’ve been talking about choices. Next, Cognitive Load, which is about everything else on the screen competing for the same brain.
