Beat 3 · At Kestrel (fictional)

The equipment form

Kestrel’s equipment form spaced every label and field exactly the same distance apart.

Labels floated between boxes. Techs typed serial numbers into the model field.

The fix: pull each label tight to its own field, and add space between pairs.

[~3 min] So here’s Kestrel’s equipment form. Kestrel’s fictional, but you’ve all seen this form somewhere. [Point to the Before screen] Every label and every field is spaced exactly the same distance apart. Very tidy. Now, quick question. [Ask the room: “Does ‘Serial number’ label the box above it, or the box below it?”] [Let people answer. You’ll usually get both answers.] Right. With identical gaps, there’s no right answer. People guess, and a fair share of them guess wrong. At Kestrel, techs kept typing serial numbers into the model field. [Point to the After screen] Now look at the fix. Nothing was added. No borders, no extra labels. Each label just moved closer to its own field, and the extra space went between the pairs. That’s it. This is one of the cheapest fixes in this whole training. And here’s the part I want engineers to hear: it usually lives in a spacing token. That makes it an engineering decision as much as a design one. Let’s talk about what each team does with this.
