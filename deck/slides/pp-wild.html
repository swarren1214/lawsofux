[~2 min] Two examples of the Pareto Principle. On the left is Microsoft’s crash reporting. Back in the early 2000s, when Windows or Office crashed, you’d get that little prompt asking if you wanted to send an error report. Microsoft analyzed those reports and found that a relatively small share of bugs caused most of the crashes. Fixing the top 20% of reported bugs removed about 80% of the errors and crashes. So they fixed the vital few first, and it had an outsized effect. On the right, support queues. This one shows up almost everywhere. If you tag support tickets for a few weeks, you’ll find that a handful of root causes are behind most of them. Fix those, and the whole queue shrinks. [Pause] The lesson from both is the same: count before you choose. Our intuition about what matters most is often wrong. These illustrations are generic sketches, by the way.
