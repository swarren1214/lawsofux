Beat 1 · The law

Von Restorff Effect

When several similar things appear together, the one that differs is the one people remember.

Von Restorff (1933): in lists of similar items, the single item that stood out was recalled far better.

Source: lawsofux.com/von-restorff-effect · von Restorff, 1933

[~3 min] The Von Restorff Effect. When several similar things show up together, the one that’s different is the one people remember. [Point to the animation] Remember the cover slide, and the glowing orange tile everyone noticed first? This is that law. Hedwig von Restorff showed in 1933 that in a list of similar items, the one item that stood out was recalled far better than its neighbors. [Pause] In interfaces, it’s the principle behind primary buttons, recommended plans and alerts. But here’s the catch: it only works by contrast. Every extra thing you highlight weakens everything else you’ve highlighted, until nothing stands out at all. I think of emphasis as a budget, and honestly, most products overspend it. [Ask the room: “Show of hands: who thinks our busiest screen has more than three things trying to be the most important?”] [Count the hands and smile.] Yeah. Let’s look at two examples that spend that budget wisely.
