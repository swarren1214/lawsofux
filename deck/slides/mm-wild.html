Design for the model they have.

[~3 min] Two examples, one where the model works and one where it doesn’t. On the left, the desktop trash can. That’s a mental model deliberately borrowed from the real world. Things you throw away go in a bin, and they stay there until you empty it. Nobody ever needed that explained. [Pause] On the right is my favorite example, the thermostat. Willett Kempton published a study in 1986 and found that a lot of people think of a thermostat like a gas pedal. Crank it up higher and the house heats faster. [Ask the room: “Be honest, who’s done this?”] [Wait for a few sheepish hands] Yeah. But most furnaces don’t work that way. They just switch on until they reach the target. Setting it higher just means you overshoot. So the person’s model is wrong, but it’s a very reasonable model. So the design lesson has two options. Either match the model people already have, like the trash can does, or make the real model visible so they can correct theirs. And quick note, these illustrations are generic sketches.
