Teach by doing.

Level one as tutorial

Super Mario Bros.’ opening level teaches jumping, enemies and power-ups through play, not a manual.

The level is the lesson.

Empty states that teach

Good empty states turn a blank screen into a first step, like “Create your first project.”

Make step one the lesson.

[~2 min] Two great examples of teaching by doing. On the left, the opening level of Super Mario Bros., from 1985. Shigeru Miyamoto has talked about designing it this way. There’s no tutorial screen. The level itself is built so the first enemy, the first block and the first power-up teach you the rules by letting you try them. You learn to jump because you have to jump. The level is the lesson. [Pause] On the right is the software version of that: empty states. The very first time someone shows up with nothing yet, a good empty state doesn’t just say “No projects.” It shows the one action that gets them started, like “Create your first project.” Step one becomes the lesson. Quick note, these are generic sketches, not the actual game or any real product. [Ask the room: “What does a brand-new user see on our most important screen?
