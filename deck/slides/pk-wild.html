[~2 min] Two sides of Parkinson’s Law. On the left, the user side: browser autofill. You know that checkout form with your name, address and card number? Typing it all out takes a few minutes. With autofill, it’s one tap and a few seconds. And every second we save is a second less for doubt, distraction or giving up. That’s what designing for the minimum time looks like. On the right, the team side: timeboxed sprints. Scrum teams work in fixed-length sprints, and the length doesn’t change. What changes is the scope. So the work flexes to fit the time, instead of the time quietly stretching to fit the work. That’s the classic countermeasure to Parkinson’s Law. Fix the time, flex the scope.
